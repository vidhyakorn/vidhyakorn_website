## Abstract
This conceptual model frames neurodevelopmental conditions (NDCs) including Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD), Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), Down Syndrome and Learning Disabilities (LD) as dynamically interconnected systems shaped across the life-course. Grounded in the NeuroDiversity LifeSpace (NDLS) framework, it views individuals as autonomous agents continually evolving through the co-constitution of their biology, cognition, and environment. To operationalize this framework, the model proposes integrating longitudinal causal analysis of linked population data alongside agent-based modeling (ABM). This combined methodological approach enables the dynamic simulation of vulnerable populations, rigorously capturing how individual, family, school, and neighborhood contexts interact to shape socio-educational trajectories over time. Applying the capabilities approach as a normative framework, the model investigates how varying socio-economic resources and structural barriers across these systemic levels impair or support human functioning. Ultimately, the NDLS framework provides an empirical, spatiotemporal pathway to test causal hypotheses regarding neurodevelopmental variability, aiming to guide targeted, capability-based policy interventions that foster equitable human development.
